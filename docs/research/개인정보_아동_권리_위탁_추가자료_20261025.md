## Reviewed Q&A anchors

### Child under 14: consent and legal representative

**Answer supported:** When consent under the Act is required to process a child's personal information, Article 22-2(1) requires the legal representative's consent and requires the controller to verify that consent. Under Article 22-2(2), the minimum information prescribed by Presidential Decree that is needed to obtain that consent may be collected directly from the child without the legal representative's consent. Article 22-2(3) requires notices to children under 14 about processing to use an easily understood format and clear, accessible language. The direct-collection detail is subject to the decree; do not generalize it beyond the statutory language. **Anchor:** `art22-2`, paragraphs 1-3.

### Access requests and refusal/limitation

**Answer supported:** Article 35(1) provides the data subject's right to request access to their own information; paragraph 2 provides the public-body route. Under Article 35(3), the controller must provide access within the period set by Presidential Decree. A justified reason can support postponement during that period if the reason is communicated; access must follow without delay once it ends. Under paragraph 4, access may be restricted or refused only in the specified cases: a statutory prohibition/restriction; risk of harm to another person's life/body or unfair infringement of another person's property or other interests; or serious interference with a public body's enumerated work. The reason must be communicated. Do not state a precise deadline from this Act alone or describe exceptions as open-ended discretion. **Anchor:** `art35`, paragraphs 1-5.

### Correction and erasure; statutory collection requirement

**Answer supported:** Article 36(1) allows a person who has accessed their information under Article 35 to request correction or erasure. Its proviso bars an erasure request where another statute specifies that information as a collection target. Under Article 36(2), absent a special procedure in another law, the controller investigates without delay, takes necessary action according to the request, and informs the person of the result. Deleted information must not be recoverable or reproducible (paragraph 3); if the proviso applies, the controller must promptly notify the requester (paragraph 4). Do not broaden the proviso into a blanket claim that any general retention period automatically defeats erasure; identify whether another statute specifies the information as a collection target and whether it supplies a special procedure. **Anchor:** `art36`, paragraphs 1-6.

### Suspension/withdrawal versus erasure

**Answer supported:** Article 37(1) allows a request to suspend processing or withdrawal of consent, with a specific limitation for public bodies to registered files under Article 32. Under paragraph 2, the controller must promptly suspend all or part of processing, but may refuse a suspension request only on the listed grounds: a specific statute or unavoidable statutory duty; risk to another person's life/body or unfair infringement of another person's interests; a public body's inability to perform its statutory function without processing; or inability to perform an agreed service contract where the person has not clearly indicated intent to terminate it. When consent is withdrawn, paragraph 3 generally requires promptly taking necessary steps, including destruction that prevents recovery/reproduction, subject to those same grounds. Under paragraph 5, information whose processing has been suspended must be promptly destroyed or otherwise handled as necessary. Suspension/withdrawal is distinct from an erasure request under Article 36, although the Act can require destruction in these circumstances. Reasons for a refusal or omission of withdrawal steps must be notified promptly (paragraph 4). **Anchor:** `art37`, paragraphs 1-6.

### Outsourcing versus third-party provision

**Answer supported:** Article 26(1) requires a document containing the listed matters when a controller entrusts processing to a third party; those include purpose-limited processing, safeguards, and matters prescribed by Presidential Decree. The Act says a document, so avoid claiming a particular contract format unless another rule requires it. Paragraph 2 requires public disclosure of the entrusted work and processor in a method set by Presidential Decree. Paragraph 3 separately requires notice for outsourced promotion or sales solicitation. Paragraph 4 requires training and supervision of the processor. Paragraph 5 prohibits the processor from using or providing information beyond the entrusted work's scope. Paragraph 6 requires the entrusting party's consent before re-entrustment. Paragraph 7 deems the processor an employee of the controller for damages caused by the processor's violation in handling personal information for the entrusted work. Paragraph 8 applies enumerated provisions to the processor, treating it as controller for those provisions. This is entrusted processing; it does not automatically establish a legal basis for an independent third-party provision. If the recipient independently receives/uses the information, separately assess Article 17 (and any other applicable basis). **Anchor:** `art26`, paragraphs 1-8; comparison with existing `art17` source.

## Exact deadlines and subordinate rules

These provisions delegate procedural details and periods to Presidential Decree. This source set does not establish an exact number of days for every procedure. Do not invent a universal processing period; check the currently effective subordinate provision and applicable procedure before stating a precise deadline.

## Review limitation

This is a text-grounded summary of the cited provisions, not a determination of any agency's particular processing purpose, applicable special statute, or case facts. Verify those facts before applying an exception or deciding whether processing must stop or information must be erased.
